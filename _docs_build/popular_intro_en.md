## 1. The Spacetime Dilemma: A Century-Long Conflict

### Two great edifices, two spacetimes

Twentieth-century physics erected two magnificent edifices.

One is called the **Standard Model**. It describes the behavior of elementary particles in the microscopic world — quarks, electrons, photons, gluons — and three of the forces between them (electromagnetic, weak, strong). The Standard Model is extraordinarily successful, accurate to more than ten decimal places, one of the most thoroughly verified theories in the history of science.

The other is called **General Relativity**. It describes gravity in the macroscopic world — stars, galaxies, black holes, the evolution of the whole universe. It is equally successful, having predicted the bending of light, gravitational waves, and cosmic expansion, all confirmed by observation.

The problem is that these two edifices hold **fundamentally contradictory** views of "spacetime."

General Relativity says: spacetime is a **curved, elastic, living thing**. Matter tells spacetime how to curve; spacetime tells matter how to move. Spacetime is itself a dynamical object — it can be bent by mass, it can ripple (gravitational waves), and it can have a beginning and an end (inside a black hole).

The Standard Model says: spacetime is just a **fixed, rigid background stage**. Particles and fields dance on this stage, and the stage itself does not move at all. It assumes a flat, uniform spacetime (physicists call it "Minkowski spacetime"), like an infinite, never-changing grid.

One says spacetime is alive; the other says spacetime is dead. These two things cannot both be true.

### First, go deeper: what actually is gravity?

Before discussing the conflict, we need to seriously ask a deceptively simple question: **what, exactly, is gravity?**

Because our answer to "what is gravity" directly determines our attitude toward "what is spacetime."

**The traditional answer (General Relativity):** gravity is not a "force" but the curvature of spacetime. The Earth orbits the Sun not because an invisible rope pulls it, but because the Sun "bends" the spacetime around it, and the Earth simply follows a "straight line" (a geodesic) in that curved spacetime. The falling apple, the bending of light, the black hole — all are manifestations of spacetime curvature.

This answer is beautiful, but it binds "gravity" to "the geometry of spacetime": **gravity = spacetime curvature**. And so "spacetime" must be a real thing that can be bent.

**Another answer (the quantum field view):** gravity is carried by a particle called the "graviton," just as electromagnetism is carried by the photon. The graviton is a massless, spin-2 particle.

But this answer also has trouble: the graviton merely "quantizes gravity as if it were an ordinary force"; it does not answer the deeper question of *why* gravity exists, and its calculations diverge everywhere.

We give a **third answer** — the one running through this entire introduction: **gravity is neither spacetime curvature nor a fundamental force; it is an "emergent" phenomenon — just like temperature.**

### How temperature "emerges"

To understand "gravity emerges like temperature," we must first explain what temperature is. This may be the single most important analogy in this document, so please read it carefully.

A cup of water consists of roughly 10²³ water molecules. Each molecule is in random thermal motion — some fast, some slow, in every direction.

Now ask: **what is the "temperature" of one particular water molecule in this cup?**

The question is meaningless. **A single molecule has no temperature.** You cannot point at one molecule and say "its temperature is 30 degrees."

"Temperature" is a property that belongs only to the **collective of many molecules**:

- The greater the **average kinetic energy** of the molecules, the higher the temperature of the water;
- More precisely, temperature is defined through entropy: `1/T = ∂S/∂E` — temperature is the reciprocal of "how much the entropy (disorder, number of states) increases when you add one unit of energy";
- The higher the temperature, the less the entropy increases for the same added energy (the system is already disordered enough not to care).

So the essence of temperature is this: **it lives in no single molecule; it "arises" from the collective motion of billions upon billions of molecules.** Microscopically there is only "molecules moving"; macroscopically there is "temperature" — this arising from micro to macro is what physics calls **emergence**.

Emergence has a distinctive feature: **you cannot find it microscopically, yet macroscopically it exists truly and lawfully.** Cut open a cup of water and you will never find a "temperature" in there; but put your hand in and you will truly feel it.

## 2. The Foundation Problem of Quantum Field Theory: Where Does the Measure Come From?

### How quantum field theory is "assembled"

The Standard Model is a **quantum field theory**. Quantum field theory has a standard assembly procedure, which can be roughly divided into three steps:

1. **Define the field:** first assume that at every point of space there is a "field" (the electromagnetic field, the electron field, …). The field is a quantity pervading spacetime.
2. **Write down the path integral:** to compute the probability for "a particle to go from here to there," physicists sum over all possible "paths" (histories), each path contributing a weight. This is Feynman's path integral.
3. **Provide the measure:** this act of "summing over all paths" needs a "ruler" to measure different paths — which path counts as one unit, and how it is weighted. This ruler is mathematically called the **measure**.

### The measure is the foundation, and the foundation depends on spacetime

The third step is the key, and the hardest.

What is the measure? It is a metric on the infinite-dimensional space of "all possible field configurations." And what is a "field configuration"? It is a quantity **defined at every point and every instant of spacetime**.

That is: **the measure inherently depends on spacetime.** To measure a field, you must first lay down a grid over spacetime and integrate the field's values at every lattice point. Spacetime is the stage on which the measure is defined.

How hard this step is, one can feel it: the path integral sums over "infinitely many possible field configurations," and each configuration is "values at infinitely many points of spacetime." That is an "infinity of infinities" — two layers of infinity stacked on each other. To this day no one has rigorously and uncontroversially defined this sum. This is not technical laziness; it is a **fundamental difficulty**.

### Then the contradiction surfaces

Now bring in the conclusion of Chapter 1:

- spacetime does not exist (it is only a description of change);
- yet the measure of quantum field theory must depend on spacetime to be defined.

Does the measure still have a foundation? **No.**

It is like this: to measure the flow of a river, you must first assume a riverbed exists; but we have just proven the riverbed does not exist. Then with what do you measure the "flow"?

This is the deepest "foundation problem" of quantum field theory: **it stands on a foundation that does not exist.** The so-called "rigorous mathematical construction of quantum field theory remains incomplete," the so-called "path-integral measure is ill-defined" — all these unresolved difficulties trace back to one and the same fact — **the measure needs spacetime, and spacetime does not exist.**

This is also why patching inside quantum field theory can never fill the hole. You are patching "the shape of the riverbed," while the hole is "the riverbed itself." As long as you stand on the assumption "spacetime is the background," the measure remains forever suspended.

### Our way out: not a measure, but a spectrum

Since the foundation of the measure (spacetime) does not exist, our strategy is clear: **bypass the measure.**

Do not define "fields on spacetime," do not write "path integrals on spacetime," do not seek "measures on spacetime" — instead start from "the spectrum of change" (the spectrum needs no spacetime background; it needs only "change" itself), and use the spectrum to dually rebuild everything.

This route bypasses the "measure depends on spacetime" dead end. How specifically to do it is the content of Chapters 4 and 5. Here one only needs to remember a chain of logic: **spacetime does not exist → the measure has no foundation → we must bypass the measure → start from the spectrum.**

## 3. The Deep Water of the Yang–Mills Conjecture: What Is the Nature of the Field?

### A million-dollar question

In 2000, the Clay Mathematics Institute announced seven "Millennium Prize Problems," each carrying a one-million-dollar prize. The first among them is the **Yang–Mills existence and mass gap problem**.

It asks two specific things:

1. **Existence:** can the quantum Yang–Mills theory in four-dimensional spacetime (the strong-force part of the Standard Model is precisely this) be rigorously, mathematically constructed without contradiction?
2. **Mass gap:** why do its elementary excitations (gluons) have mass — and specifically a "gap" mass, i.e. there exists a lowest non-zero mass, below which there is nothing?

More than twenty years on, these two questions still have no rigorous answer. Countless brilliant people have tried and none has fully cracked it.

### The difficulty is stuck on "the nature of the field"

Why so hard? On the surface it looks like insufficient mathematical technique. But we hold that **the real difficulty is stuck on a philosophical question: what is a field, exactly?**

The traditional approach of quantum field theory goes like this:

1. First define the "field" — a mathematical object pervading spacetime (the **container** of change);
2. Then put "change" (particles, interactions) into it — let change happen inside this container;
3. Finally use the quantization rules (path integral, measure) to make this container "move."

Notice the order: **the container first, the change after.** The field is "the box that holds change."

### Putting the cart before the horse: change is the substance

We hold that this order is **backwards**.

The correct order is reversed:

- **Change comes first; it is the substance (ontology);**
- **The field is the structure formed by change** — the relatively stable shape into which a great deal of change "freezes."

Use the river analogy: the traditional approach is "first the riverbed (field, container), then let the water (change) flow in it"; we say, **there is no riverbed at all — the water (change) flows, and as it flows it "carves out" a shape that looks like a riverbed; that shape is what we call the "field."**

An even better analogy: a crowd of people (change) walks back and forth; where many people walk, a "path" is trodden into the ground. **The path (field) is not there first; it is trodden out by the people (change).** Traditional quantum field theory is "build the road first, then let people walk"; we say "people walk, and the road emerges on its own."

One can also use music: a melody (field) is, in essence, a string of notes (change). The traditional approach is "first the melody, then let the notes fill it"; we say, **it is the notes (change) whose combination constitutes the melody (field).** The melody is not a container holding notes; the melody is itself one way of organizing the notes.

### The ontological turn: from "things that exist" to "change itself"

This is the ontological turn of our entire method:

> **Not "change happens inside the field," but "the field is generated by change." Change is the substance; the field is derivative.**

Once we accept this, the two difficulties of the Yang–Mills conjecture are recast:

In other words: **we no longer try to "directly construct the field" (that is constructing the container of change, backwards); instead we start from "change" itself, letting the field emerge as the structure of change.**

This turn is the starting point of everything that follows. It repositions the century-old difficulty of "constructing quantum field theory" from a "problem of mathematical technique" into a "problem of the starting point" — the problem is not "how to build the container," but "we should never have started from the container at all."

### The Yang–Mills conjecture: where exactly is the difficulty?

Yang–Mills theory is the mathematical skeleton of the strong force (QCD) in the Standard Model. The Clay Institute's million-dollar prize is really asking two very specific questions:

**First, existence:** can this quantum theory, in four-dimensional spacetime, be rigorously constructed without blowing up (no infinities)?

**Second, the mass gap:** why are gluons (the carriers of the strong force) forever "imprisoned" inside hadrons in experiment, so that physicists never see a free gluon? ## 5. Several Key Points of the Method

This chapter breaks our method into a few operational technical points, each corresponding to a concrete computation already implemented in code.

### Point 1: three levels of the spectrum

We distinguish three levels of the spectrum, from concrete to abstract:

1. **Discrete spectrum** (most concrete): specific quantities one by one — particle masses, energy gaps, the hierarchy of three fermion generations, the gauge coupling constants. These are what finally get compared against observation.
2. **Spectral sum** (middle level): summing over the spectrum, taking traces, computing densities — for example the "window capacity" (how many spectral modes a window can hold). This is the bridge from the discrete spectrum to the continuum limit.
3. **Continuum limit / regularisation** (most abstract): using the Weyl law, heat-kernel expansion, KK reduction, and so on, to handle the behavior of the spectrum at infinity. This is what makes the computation "convergent, non-divergent."

All three levels are indispensable: without the discrete spectrum there is nothing to compare against observation; without the spectral sum there is no internal self-consistency; without the continuum limit there is no "non-divergence." This three-layer structure corresponds to the complete chain "concrete ↔ middle ↔ abstract."

### Point 2: two-end regularisation

The spectrum "diverges" at both ends, and each end needs separate handling:

- **Ultraviolet end (UV, high frequency):** the higher the frequency, the more modes, and the sum explodes. We use a **Gaussian window** (heat kernel) to smoothly cut off the high-frequency modes — not a crude one-shot chop, but a gentle "closing of the window" with a Gaussian function. Why a Gaussian window rather than a hard cutoff? Because a hard cutoff leaves artificial "boundary marks," while the Gaussian window is natural, smooth, and leaves no artifacts.
- **Infrared end (IR, low frequency):** the lower the frequency, the larger the reach involved, and the sum extends to infinity. We use the **principle of maximal entropy** to fix the infrared end — let the entropy be maximised under the constraints.

Only when both ends are handled is the middle spectral sum well-defined. This "two-end regularisation" is the cornerstone that makes the whole method "computably convergent."

### Point 3: entropy is the bridge between UV and IR

One quantity that is unexpectedly present throughout is **entropy**.

We find that the "span" from the ultraviolet window to the infrared window, after taking its logarithm, is `∫γ_M = ln(window span) = 139.253`. This number is exactly a Boltzmann entropy `S = ln W` — where `W` is "the number of ways of change the window can hold."

**Entropy becomes the bridge connecting ultraviolet and infrared, connecting the microscopic and the macroscopic.** This is the most elegant thread in the whole method: it ties the purely counting question "how many ways can change change" to the question "what does the universe look like."

Entropy here is not the "degree of disorder" of thermodynamics, but a more universal concept: **entropy = the logarithm of the number of ways of change.** How many different ways of change this window can hold — its logarithm is the entropy. And it is precisely this entropy that welds the two extremes, the high-frequency end (Planck scale) and the low-frequency end (Hubble scale), into one body.

The highest principle running through everything, we call **"duality emergence"**:

Four dualities are unified in one framework:
